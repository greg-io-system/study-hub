1 · Factoring"Solve for all values by FACTORING." Two equations: one with just x², one with a number in front of x².

A · Just x² (nothing in front of it)

What to do
  1. Look at the last number (c) and the middle number (b), with their signs.
  2. List the pairs of numbers that multiply to c.
  3. From that list, pick the pair that adds to b.
  4. Write two brackets: (x + first number)(x + second number) = 0.
  5. Make each bracket equal 0 and solve each one. Those two x-values are the answer.
Worked twin A: x² + 3x − 18 = 0
c = −18, b = +3read the numbers with their signs
Pairs that multiply to −18: 1 & −18, −1 & 18, 2 & −9, −2 & 9, 3 & −6, −3 & 6list them all
−3 + 6 = 3 ✓the pair that adds to b
(x − 3)(x + 6) = 0one number in each bracket
x − 3 = 0 → x = 3;  x + 6 = 0 → x = −6each bracket = 0, then solve

B · A number in front of x² (the ac method)

What to do
  1. Multiply the front number (a) by the last number (c). Call it ac.
  2. Find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b (same listing trick as part A).
  3. Rewrite the equation with the middle term split into those two pieces.
  4. Split it into two pairs. From each pair, take out what both terms share. The two brackets left over must match.
  5. Write (what you took out) × (the matching bracket). Make each bracket = 0 and solve.

2 · Completing the square"Solve by COMPLETING THE SQUARE." Two equations: one has ordinary answers, one has complex (i) answers.

What to do
  1. Move the plain number to the right side (subtract it from both sides).
  2. Take the middle number (b), cut it in half, then square that. Add the result to both sides.
  3. The left side now factors into the same bracket twice: (x + half of b)(x + half of b), which is (x + half of b)².
  4. Take the square root of both sides. The square root undoes the ² on the left; on the right write ± in front.
  5. Move the number in the bracket to the right side. Then split into two answers: one with +, one with −.
Picture this — why you add (half of b)²: for x² + 10x, draw a box with x and 5 (half of 10) on each side. Three of the four pieces are already in the equation; the corner (5 × 5 = 25) is the missing piece that completes the square.

3 · Quadratic formula"Solve using the QUADRATIC FORMULA. Round answers to the nearest hundredth if necessary."

What to do

x = ( −b ± √(b² − 4ac) ) / 2a

  1. Write down a (the number in front of x²), b (the number with x) and c (the plain number), with their signs.
  2. Put them into the formula in brackets: −(b) ± √( (b)² − 4(a)(c) ) over 2(a).
  3. Under the square root: square b, then multiply 4 × a × c, then subtract. On the bottom: multiply 2 × a.
  4. If the number under the root is a perfect square (4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64...): take its root, then do + and − as two answers. Round only if an answer doesn't come out exact.
  5. If it isn't: simplify the root by splitting it into (perfect square) × (what's left), e.g. √44 = √4 · √11 = 2√11. Then divide every number on top by the bottom number.
  6. The sheet says round: use a calculator for the root, do + and −, round to 2 decimal places.

4 · Graph from standard form"Graph the function f(x) = x² + bx + c and determine": y-intercept, axis of symmetry, x-intercepts, vertex, factored form, vertex form.

First: what are you looking for?

The graph of x² + bx + c is a U-shaped curve (a parabola). Every one of the sheet's six features is either a point or a line on that U, or a different way of writing the same function:

FeatureWhat it is on the graph
y-interceptWhere the U crosses the up-and-down line (the y-axis). Every point on that line has x = 0.
Axis of symmetryThe dashed up-and-down line through the middle. Fold the graph along it and the two halves match. Written as x = a number.
x-interceptsWhere the U crosses the side-to-side line (the x-axis). Every point on that line has y = 0. Usually two of them.
VertexThe bottom point of the U (where it turns around). It sits on the axis of symmetry.
Factored formThe function written as two brackets, (x − p)(x − q). Not a point: it's the same function rewritten, and it shows the x-intercepts.
Vertex formThe function written as (x − h)² + k. Same function again, rewritten so it shows the vertex (h, k).

How to find each one (in the sheet's order)

  1. y-intercept: put 0 in for x. Every x-term becomes 0, so you're left with the plain number c. Write it as a point: (0, c).
  2. Axis of symmetry: use x = −b / (2a): flip the sign of b, divide by 2 times a. Write it as x = ___.
  3. x-intercepts: set the function = 0 and factor it (problem 1, part A). Each bracket = 0 gives one x. Write each as a point: (x, 0).
  4. Vertex: its x is the axis number from step 2. Put that number into the function in place of x to get its y. Write (x, y).
  5. Factored form: the two brackets from step 3.
  6. Vertex form: start from (x − h)² + k and put in the vertex: h = the vertex's x, k = the vertex's y. Clean up the signs.
Worked twin: f(x) = x² − 6x + 8  (a = 1, b = −6, c = 8)
y-intercept: f(0) = 0 − 0 + 8 = 8 → (0, 8)step 1: just the c
Axis of symmetry: x = −(−6) / (2 × 1) = 6 / 2 → x = 3step 2: −(−6) flips to +6
x-intercepts: x² − 6x + 8 = 0; multiply to 8, add to −6: −2 and −4 → (x − 2)(x − 4) = 0 → x = 2, x = 4 → (2, 0) and (4, 0)step 3: factor, each bracket = 0
Vertex: f(3) = 3² − 6(3) + 8 = 9 − 18 + 8 = −1 → (3, −1)step 4: put the axis number 3 in for x

Vertex form: h = 3, k = −1 → (x − 3)² + (−1) = (x − 3)² − 1step 6. Check: (x − 3)² − 1 = x² − 6x + 9 − 1 = x² − 6x + 8 ✓

How to draw it on the grid

  1. Plot the vertex (3, −1): go right 3, down 1.
  2. Draw the axis of symmetry as a dashed up-and-down line through x = 3.
  3. Plot the x-intercepts (2, 0) and (4, 0) on the side-to-side axis.
  4. Plot the y-intercept (0, 8). Then plot its mirror point: (0, 8) is 3 steps left of the dashed line, so go 3 steps right of it: (6, 8).
  5. Join the points with a smooth U, curving through the vertex (not a sharp V).
Memory trick: the axis of symmetry is always halfway between the two x-intercepts: (2 + 4) / 2 = 3. A quick check on step 2. And if the U looks lopsided, make a little table: put x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 into the function (you get 3, 0, −1, 0, 3) and plot those.

5 · Graph from factored form"Graph the function f(x) = (x + p)(x + q) and determine": x-intercepts, axis of symmetry, vertex, y-intercept, standard form, vertex form.

Same U, same features as problem 4 — this time you start from the brackets, so the x-intercepts come first.

What to do (in the sheet's order)
  1. x-intercepts: make each bracket = 0 and solve. The sign flips: (x + 2) gives x = −2. Write each as (x, 0).
  2. Axis of symmetry: halfway between the two x-intercepts: add them and divide by 2. Write x = ___.
  3. Vertex: put the axis number into the brackets in place of x and multiply. Write (x, y).
  4. y-intercept: put 0 in for x and multiply the two brackets. Write (0, y).
  5. Standard form: multiply the brackets out (FOIL: first, outer, inner, last), then combine the two x-terms.
  6. Vertex form: (x − h)² + k with the vertex from step 3 (same as problem 4, step 6).

8 · Complex numbers"Simplify each expression. If possible write your answer as a complex number (a + bi)." Two multiplications, two distribute-and-subtract, two powers of i.

The one rule: i² = −1. Treat i like x. When you're done multiplying, replace every i² with −1. Write the plain number first, then the i part: a + bi.
What to do
  1. Two brackets multiplied: FOIL — multiply first × first, outer × outer, inner × inner, last × last. You get 4 terms.
  2. A number or i outside a bracket: multiply it by each term inside.
  3. A minus sign in front of a bracket: flip the sign of both terms inside.
  4. Replace each i² with −1 (so 12i² becomes −12, and −15i² becomes +15).
  5. Add the plain numbers together; add the i terms together.
FOIL: (5 − 3i)(2 − 4i)
F: 5 × 2 = 10   O: 5 × (−4i) = −20i
I: (−3i) × 2 = −6i   L: (−3i)(−4i) = 12i²

12i² = 12(−1) = −12
plain: 10 − 12 = −2;  i: −20i − 6i = −26i
= −2 − 26i
Distribute, then a minus bracket: 3i(2 − 5i) − (4 − 2i)
3i × 2 = 6i;  3i × (−5i) = −15i²
−(4 − 2i) = −4 + 2i  (both signs flip)
6i − 15i² − 4 + 2i
−15i² = −15(−1) = +15
plain: 15 − 4 = 11;  i: 6i + 2i = 8i
= 11 + 8i
Minus a distributed bracket: (1 − 4i) − 2i(3 − 6i)

Memory trick: "i, minus one, minus i, one" — then it starts over, like the four seasons.

9 · Square roots of negatives"Write the simplified form of each expression, expressing your answer in terms of i when applicable." Single roots, a number times a root, two roots multiplied, and four-term sums.

What to do
  1. If there's a minus sign inside the root, take it out as an i: √−50 becomes i√50.
  2. Find the biggest perfect square that divides the number (4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 100). Split the root: √50 = √25 · √2.

  4. If there are numbers outside already, multiply them all together. Write the i in front of the √.
  5. Two roots multiplied: simplify each one first (steps 1-3), then multiply outside × outside and inside × inside.
  6. A sum of four roots: simplify each root, then add the plain numbers together and the i terms together.
