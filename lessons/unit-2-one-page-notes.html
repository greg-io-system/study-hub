Your notes, on one page

How to fit Unit 2 onto one sheet you can actually use during the test, and how to decide what goes on it.

The test: 35 multiple choice, about 12 of them synthesis questions (they ask you to connect or compare). You can use any notes in your own handwriting, and you hand them in. Questions also come from OpenStax 8.2 and 9.2. Bring a district Chromebook.
Why one page: his study guide says you can use any handwritten notes; it doesn't mention a page limit. One page is your choice, and a good one: it's faster to write tonight, and in the test you find a name in seconds instead of flipping through four pages while the clock runs.
Draw the grid by hand. Only handwritten notes are allowed, so don't print a template: rule the lines yourself and write every label. It takes two minutes.

1. The layout: one grid, not a list

Turn the page sideways. Make a row for each civilization and a column for each kind of fact. A list makes you read top to bottom to find something; a grid lets you go straight to "India, beliefs." It also does half the work for the synthesis questions: to compare Rome and Han, you read down one column.

3. The filter: how to keep it to one page

Rule 1. Clues, not sentences

Your notes only have to remind you; the question supplies the rest. Aim for 3 to 6 words per term.

Too long (a whole note)Darius made the government stronger by splitting Persia into 20 provinces, the satrapies. A governor called a satrap ran each one: he collected the taxes, kept order, handled justice and raised soldiers for the army.
One-page versionSatrapy = Darius's 20 provinces. Satrap = governor: taxes, justice, troops.

That example is from Mr. Angelopulos's Persia slides. Use symbols to save space: = for "is," → for "led to," vs for a contrast, ↑/↓ for rise/fall.

Rule 2. Skip what you know cold

If you'd get it right without notes, it doesn't need room. Go down the checklist and tick the terms you're sure of: write those as a single word or leave them out. Spend the space on the ones you'd blank on, and above all the ★ pairs.

Rule 3. Give the mix-ups their own box

Multiple choice loves near-twins: four answers that all sound right. Put the ★ groups in the "Mix-ups" corner, each with the one detail that tells them apart, from your notes:

  • Cyrus / Cambyses II / Darius I, and the four capitals
  • Socrates / Plato / Aristotle; Marathon / Thermopylae
  • Legalism / Confucianism / Daoism; Qin / Han
  • Julius Caesar / Augustus; Republic / Empire
  • Chandragupta / Ashoka; Mauryan / Gupta; Brahma / Brahman

Rule 4. The synthesis questions want comparisons

About a third of the test connects things. You've already done the thinking in two assignments: your Han China vs. Rome one-pager and your Caste and Class chart. Copy your best one line from each into the "Compare" box. Then add one line for each "same idea, different place" you can see down a column of your grid, for example:

  • How did each empire hold a huge territory together? (the "How it was ruled" column)
  • What weakened or ended each empire? (Look along your "rise & fall" column: what do your notes say about each event there?)
  • What connected the regions? (the Silk Road and the Indian Ocean network)

These are questions for you to answer from your notes, not answers.

Rule 5. Don't skip the two readings

He said plainly there will be questions from OpenStax 8.2 and 9.2. Give each 3 or 4 lines in the bottom strip.

4. Tonight, in about an hour

  1. 5 min: rule the grid sideways on one sheet and write the labels.
  2. 15 min: go down the checklist above and tick what you know cold.
  3. 30 min: fill the cells from your class notes, clues only, starting with the ★ terms. Then the bottom strip.
  4. 5 min, the speed test: pick five terms at random from the checklist. Can you find each one on your sheet in under 10 seconds? If not, it's in the wrong cell or written too small.
In the testFind the name or term in the question, go to its row, then read across. For a "compare" question, read down the column instead.
