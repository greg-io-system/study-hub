Tissue Box Project — Guide

Due Wednesday 8:00 AM. Box gets built in class Tue/Wed — this guide is for getting your plan finished first, so class time is just decorating. Gallery Walk on the due date, extra credit for the best box.

What's being graded — 10 points per side, 40 total

Each of the four sides (epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous) needs:

  • Name of the tissue type (1 pt)
  • Two pictures: a microscope-level picture of the tissue (1 pt) AND a body picture showing where it's found (1 pt)
  • Descriptions: structures (1 pt), functions (2 pts), subtypes (2 pts), characteristics (2 pts)

Also in the instructions: your name goes on the bottom, the top/opening side stays blank, and each side should be neat, colorful, and accurate. The full instructions and rubric are in your project doc in Google Classroom.

Which row does a fact go in? (the decoder)

The rubric doesn't define its four labels — use these questions to sort any fact into the right row:

  • Structures — what would you see through the microscope? What the tissue is made of and how its cells are arranged (tightly packed cells; fibers + ground substance; long muscle fibers; neurons with dendrites, cell body, axon).
  • Functions — what job does it do for the body? Protect, connect, move, communicate.
  • Subtypes — what named kinds of it exist? Squamous/cuboidal/columnar; bone, blood, cartilage, adipose; skeletal/cardiac/smooth; neurons and glial cells.
  • Characteristics — what's left that makes this tissue different from the other three? Has blood vessels or not, regrows or not, voluntary or not, "90% of cancers start here."

Watch out: "found in the brain and spinal cord" is a location — that's your macro-picture answer, not a structure. If a fact answers "where," it goes with the body picture.
