The Layers of Skin

Skin isn't one thing — it's a stack. Tap a layer in the diagram to see what's in it, then work down through the five layers of the epidermis.

The three main layers, outermost first:
  • Epidermis — the outer layer, made of 5 strata. About .05 mm thick on the eyelids up to 1.5 mm on the feet.
  • Dermis — the middle layer (1–4 mm) where "all the goodies live": glands, hair follicles, receptors, blood vessels, nerves.
  • Hypodermis (subcutaneous layer) — attaches the dermis to muscle and bone; has a layer of adipose (fat) for insulation and protection.

    The four epidermis cells to know

    • Keratinocytes — make keratin proteins that give skin, hair and nails strength and structure. Keratinocytes in the stratum basale divide (her burn-healing slide: that's how first-degree burns heal).
    • Melanocytes — in the stratum basale. Melanogenesis: they produce melanin pigments, which give skin its color.
    • Merkel cells — in the stratum basale. Special skin receptors that sense light touch, pressure and fine textures.
    • Langerhans cells — in the stratum spinosum. Important for immunity: they tell dangerous cells from cells that aren't dangerous. Her functions slide notes they come from bone marrow.

    Structures of the dermis (your Skin Diagram)

    The labels on Mrs. Kalec's Skin Diagram template:

    • Hair: hair shaft, hair root, hair follicle, arrector pili muscle
    • Glands: eccrine sweat gland, pore of sweat gland, sebaceous gland

    One more label on the template is hard to read (it ends in "...follicle ... receptor") — check the exact name on your own Skin Diagram.

    What her dermis slide says it contains: sweat glands; corpuscles that feel pressure; pain, pleasure and itch receptors; hair follicles; the arrector pili muscle; blood vessels and nerves. The dermis also makes and secretes oils that keep skin smooth and soft.

    What skin does (her functions slide)

    • Regulates body temperature — through sweat, and muscle contraction (blood vessels, arrector pili muscles).
    • Protection — a physical barrier against abrasion, bacteria, dehydration and UV light.
    • Sensation — pain, pressure and temperature, through receptors.
    • Excretion — wastes such as ions, electrolytes, urea and amino acids.
    • Immunity — Langerhans cells (from bone marrow); her slide lists them with helper-T cells and antibodies.
    • Blood reservoir — with moderate exercise, blood vessels expand and heat leaves; with strenuous exercise, they contract and send blood to the muscles.
    • Vitamin D synthesis — triggered by UV light; vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium to build bone and healthy tissue.

    Check yourself

    1Name the three main layers of skin, starting at the surface.

    Show answer
    Epidermis → dermis → hypodermis (subcutaneous layer).

    2List the five strata of the epidermis, starting with the outermost.

    Show answer
    Corneum → Lucidum → Granulosum → Spinosum → Basale. ("Creamy Lotion Gets Skin Beautiful.")

    3What is the stratum corneum made of, and how often is it replaced?

    Show answer
    Thin squamous cells that are dead — their cytoplasm has been replaced by keratin and they've lost moisture. They're shed and replaced, and the whole layer is replaced about once a month.

    4Which layer contains the arrector pili muscle, hair follicles, sweat glands and pressure-sensing corpuscles?

    Show answer
    The dermis.

    5In which stratum does keratinization begin — and which stratum is the waterproof barrier?

    Show answer
    Both: the stratum granulosum.

    6Which cells are important for immunity, and which stratum are they in?

    Show answer
    Langerhans cells, in the stratum spinosum. They tell dangerous cells from not-dangerous ones.

    7What do Merkel cells detect, and where are they?

    Show answer
    Light touch, pressure and fine textures. They're in the stratum basale.

    8Which stratum looks clear, is filled with eledin, and is the barrier between living and dead cells?

    Show answer
    The stratum lucidum (3–5 cells of dead keratinocytes).

    9Which pigment cell lives in the stratum basale, and what does melanogenesis produce?

    Show answer
    Melanocytes. Melanogenesis produces melanin pigments, which give skin its color.

    10Give two jobs of the hypodermis and two things it contains.

    Show answer
    It attaches the dermis to muscles and bones, and its adipose gives insulation and protection. It contains a layer of adipose and bigger blood vessels and nerves.
