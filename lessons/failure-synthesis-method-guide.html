Failure Synthesis — a method guide

One move: understand Leak's position, then move to yours. His 9/17 email: "this 'move' will create a layer known as sophistication."

The prompt (his 9/17 email): "What is your position with regards to Ryan Leak's position on failure?"

His note: "I am not asking you to agree or disagree with Ryan Leak and this video; in fact, an elementary view to this video would only subscribe to simple agreement or disagreement." The task is to understand Leak's position with regards to ________, "AND THEN you can move to your position with regards to an established position."

He gave the prompt first on purpose: "read the prompt. Then read the sources with the prompt in mind. Then write to the prompt."

1 · Path 1 vs. Path 2 (the shift he's asking for)

His 9/17 email: "I am in the process of shifting your writing from Path 1 to Path 2. Work to experiment with this move. You might even fail..."

Path 1
"a response that is about the sources."
Path 2
"a response that articulates YOUR POSITION and the sources are used to support YOUR position. This response would include and focus on YOUR exigence and curiosity."

And from the 9/21 class, answering a student who asked about position vs. opinion:

  • Opinion: "What you think."
  • Position: "What you think USING evidence. In other words, explain why you think what you think while also acknowledging that there are other ways that others may think."

His 9/17 email also says: "This is a response based on sources, not on opinions."

3 · The synthesis move: put a source in conversation with the anchor

The Path 2 move: take a specific idea from one source, aim it at the anchor's position, and end on your sentence. Here it is on a made-up pair on a different topic, so the method is visible and your real sources stay yours:

Made-up example · invented anchor
"Never Skip a Day," Coach Pat Morrow (invented talk): argues that a musician should practice every single day, with no rest days, because one skipped day becomes a habit.
Made-up example · invented second source
"The Garden Needs Winter," Lee Tran (invented essay): compares a mind to a garden that grows back stronger after a season of rest.
Made-up example · worked synthesis

Morrow treats a skipped day as the first crack in a habit, but Tran's garden quietly changes the question: if growth comes back after a season of rest, then rest is not the opposite of practice but one of its stages. Put next to Tran, Morrow's "never skip" starts to sound like a rule about fear of quitting rather than a rule about getting better. Where I land: a planned rest day is part of the practice, while an unplanned one is the habit Morrow is right to worry about.

See the shape: specific idea from source B → aimed at the anchor's position → last sentence is clearly mine, and it still acknowledges what the anchor gets right.

Try it on your real sources

Run the same move with Fratto, Gladwell and Whitehead, each aimed at Leak. Guiding questions — answers are yours to write:

  • What does this source let you say about Leak's position that you couldn't say from Leak alone?
  • Does it support Leak, complicate him, or do both? Where on your continuum does it land?
  • After all three, what is YOUR position on Leak's position? End each paragraph on your sentence, not theirs.
