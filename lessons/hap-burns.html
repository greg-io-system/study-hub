Burns: How Deep, and How They Heal

The four types of burns, how deep each one reaches, how each one heals — plus scars, infection, and burn myths.

The one idea to hold onto: a burn is classified by how deep it goes. Depth decides what it looks like, how much it hurts, and whether the skin can heal on its own or needs a skin graft. A 1st-degree burn heals by keratinocyte division in the stratum basale. A 3rd- or 4th-degree burn can't heal on its own, because it destroys critical structures like melanocytes, hair follicles and sweat glands — so a graft is needed to restore the skin's barrier.

The four types — tap to see the depth

Memory trick — pain is the clue: 1st degree hurts, and 2nd degree hurts severely. A 3rd-degree burn has little to no pain — not because it's mild, but because of nerve damage. Less pain here means a deeper burn.

The healing process

What happens in a burn

  1. Inflammation starts right away. Blood vessels dilate and white blood cells arrive to prevent infection.
  2. Rebuilding. Fibroblasts in the dermis make collagen, which rebuilds tissue and helps close the wound.

How each type heals

  • 1st degree: 3–7 days, on its own

    Keratinocytes in the stratum basale divide.

  • 2nd degree: 2–3 weeks, on its own

    Both the epidermis and parts of the dermis have to regenerate. Often leaves a permanent scar.

  • 3rd and 4th degree: can't heal on their own

    Critical structures are lost — melanocytes, hair follicles and sweat glands. A skin graft is needed to restore the skin's barrier function. A 4th-degree burn needs immediate medical intervention and often leaves permanent damage.

Scars and infection

  • Scars

    2nd-degree and deeper burns often leave permanent scars. A hypertrophic scar forms when fibroblasts make too much collagen during healing.

  • Infection risk

    The skin is a physical barrier against bacteria (one of its protection jobs). When a burn breaks the epidermis barrier, bacteria can get in, so infection risk jumps sharply.

    Signs of infection: more redness, pus, fever, and red streaks spreading out from the wound.

Check yourself

1What decides whether a burn is 1st, 2nd, 3rd or 4th degree?

Show answer
How deep it goes. 1st: epidermis only. 2nd: through the epidermis into the dermis. 3rd: the whole epidermis and dermis, reaching the subcutaneous layer. 4th: past the skin into muscle, tendons and bone.

2A burn has blisters, swelling and severe pain. What type is it, and how does it heal?

Show answer
2nd degree. It heals on its own in 2–3 weeks; both the epidermis and parts of the dermis have to regenerate. It often leaves a permanent scar.

3Why can a 3rd-degree burn have little to no pain?

Show answer
Nerve damage. It's a sign of a deeper burn, not a milder one.

4How long does a 1st-degree burn take to heal, and how?

Show answer
Usually 3–7 days, by keratinocyte division in the stratum basale.

5Why can't 3rd- and 4th-degree burns heal on their own, and what does a skin graft do?

Show answer
They destroy critical structures such as melanocytes, hair follicles and sweat glands. A skin graft restores the skin's barrier function.

6What happens in the inflammation phase, and when does it start?

Show answer
It starts immediately. Blood vessels dilate and white blood cells arrive to prevent infection.

7What do fibroblasts do in healing, and how are they linked to a hypertrophic scar?

Show answer
Fibroblasts in the dermis make collagen to rebuild tissue and help close the wound. A hypertrophic scar forms when they make too much collagen.

8Why does a burn raise the risk of infection, and what are the signs?

Show answer
It breaks the epidermis barrier, so bacteria can get in. Signs: more redness, pus, fever, and red streaks spreading from the wound.

9Someone says to put butter, or ice, on a burn. What's wrong with each?

Show answer
Butter or oil traps heat and raises infection risk. Ice damages the tissue further — use cool running water instead.

10True or false: all burns heal without scars.

Show answer
False. 2nd-degree and deeper burns often leave permanent scars.
