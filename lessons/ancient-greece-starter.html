City-States: Sparta, Athens, Olympia

In your textbook · 6.2

Greece wasn't one country. It was many independent city-states, each called a polis, with its own government and religious cults. They shared a language and religion but fought each other constantly. Athens became a democracy, Sparta a military state with two kings. Olympia wasn't a city-state like those two; it was a sacred site to Zeus where the Games were held.

Questions to chase
  • What was a Spartan childhood like, for boys AND girls?
  • Who actually got to vote in Athens, and who didn't?
  • Who were the helots, and why did Sparta depend on them?
Search: polis helots Cleisthenes

Ancient Olympics

In your textbook · 6.2 (boxed feature)

Held every four years at Olympia in honor of Zeus, traditionally starting in 776 BCE. Athletes came from all over the Greek world. The events trained skills for war. The Games ran for over a thousand years before a Roman emperor stopped them.

Questions to chase
  • What events were held, and who could compete?
  • What did winners get?
  • Why did the Games end in 393 CE?
Search: Olympia Zeus Theodosius

Philosophy

In your textbook · 6.2 + 6.3

Greek thinkers tried to explain the world with reason instead of myth, then turned to big questions about right and wrong. The famous chain is teacher to student: Socrates → Plato → Aristotle. Later, after Alexander, new schools asked how to live a calm, happy life.

Questions to chase
  • Why was Socrates put on trial, and what happened?
  • Where did Plato and Aristotle disagree?
  • What's the difference between a Stoic and an Epicurean?
Search: Plato's Academy Lyceum Stoicism sophists

The Greco-Persian War (Thermopylae, Marathon)

In your textbook · 6.2

The giant Persian Empire (the one you just studied) invaded Greece twice. The first time, Athens won at Marathon (490 BCE). The second time, a small force mostly of Spartans held the pass at Thermopylae (480 BCE) to buy time, then the Greek fleet won at Salamis.

Questions to chase
  • Why did Persia want to punish Athens in the first place?
  • What were the Spartans at Thermopylae actually trying to do? (Hint: not win.)
  • What does the modern "marathon" race have to do with this battle?
Search: Darius Xerxes Leonidas Salamis Herodotus

Greek Mythology and Religion

Mostly research · textbook touches it

Greeks worshipped a family of gods led by Zeus, believed to live on Mount Olympus. Each polis had its own religious cults and built temples for the gods (the Parthenon in Athens was dedicated to Athena). Big shared sacred sites included Delphi, home of Apollo's oracle.

Questions to chase
  • Who were the twelve Olympians (your class presentations name four), and what did each one rule over?
  • What did people ask the Oracle at Delphi, and how did it answer?
  • How did religion and city life mix (festivals, temples, sacrifices)?
Search: Twelve Olympians Oracle of Delphi Pythia

Architecture and Innovation

Partly in your textbook · 6.2

The Parthenon on the Acropolis in Athens is the famous one, rebuilt after the Persians destroyed the old temples. Your textbook has a twist: it was paid for with money from Athens' allies, which angered some of the other Greek city-states. "Innovation" can also mean the Greek alphabet (they added vowels) or coins (a practice Athens borrowed from the kings of Lydia).

Questions to chase
  • What styles and building methods did Greek architects use?
  • How was the Parthenon built and decorated?
  • Which Greek inventions or ideas are still in use?
Search: Parthenon Acropolis Greek alphabet vowels

Greek Theater

In your textbook · 6.2

Athens was the birthplace of theater, with masks and music. Tragedies (Sophocles, Euripides) used old myths to ask hard questions. In Aristophanes' comedy Lysistrata, the women of Greece plot to end the Peloponnesian War.

Questions to chase
  • Where and when were plays performed, and who performed them?
  • Why masks?
  • What is Antigone about, and why is it still performed?
Search: Sophocles Euripides Aristophanes Lysistrata

Foundations of Science and Math

Research-only · barely in your textbook

The textbook mentions thinkers who explained the universe without gods: Democritus said everything is made of tiny "atoms." Mr. Angelopulos's notes name Pythagoras (with Socrates and Hippocrates) among the Greeks who sought a rational, non-religious explanation for the world. After Alexander, Alexandria in Egypt had the largest collection of books in the ancient world. Which mathematicians and scientists to follow is up to your research.

Questions to chase
  • Which Greek mathematicians and scientists can you find, and what did they figure out?
  • Which of their ideas did you use in a math class this year?
  • What was the library at Alexandria?
Search: Democritus Pythagoras Alexandria

Medicine

Research-only · not in your textbook

The name to start with is Hippocrates, who is in Mr. Angelopulos's notes: with Socrates and Pythagoras, he sought a rational, non-religious explanation for the world. What that meant for medicine is your research. (Good one if you like Anatomy.)

Questions to chase
  • How did Greek doctors explain sickness?
  • What did Greek doctors contribute, and does any of it carry into medicine today?
Search: Hippocrates Greek medicine

Alexander the Great

In your textbook · 6.3

King of Macedon at 20. He conquered the whole Persian Empire, reaching as far as the Indus River (modern Pakistan), then died at 33. He left no plan for who would rule next, so his generals split the empire. His real legacy was spreading Greek culture across a huge area.

Questions to chase
  • Why did his army refuse to keep going in India?
  • Why did Alexander's forces burn Persepolis, and was Thaïs to blame? (Your textbook gives both sides.)
  • What does "Hellenistic" mean?
Search: Philip II Gaugamela Alexandria Hellenistic

Epic Hero Tales

Mostly research · textbook touches it

The two big ones are the Iliad (its hero is Achilles) and the Odyssey, credited to the poet Homer. They were sung aloud for generations before anyone wrote them down. Alexander the Great often compared himself to Achilles and claimed to be descended from him.

Questions to chase
  • What makes someone a Greek "hero," and how is that different from a superhero?
  • What happens in the Iliad and the Odyssey?
  • Was Homer a real person? (Historians aren't sure.)
Search: Iliad Odyssey Homer oral tradition
