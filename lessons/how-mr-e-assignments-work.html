How Mr. Evans's assignments work

His homework emails from Sep 10–14 share a shape. Learn the shape and the assignment is easier to read — including the thing he warns about: notes of summary.

The big idea: the lettered task is not the only part that matters. Around it, he names the tools to use (the They Say / I Say templates, the Glossary of Terms) and then asks you to reflect on whether you used them. Read the whole email, not just the letters.

Five moves in these emails

1
Read it all. His homework emails open "To a candid class," with a note to the class before the task — and he tells you that part matters.

"Much learning is available between classes through email, please read carefully and fully as there is significant context, feedback, and opportunity for curiosity in the paragraphs to come." ("For Monday (9/14)" email, sent 9/11)

Your move: read the whole email before doing anything.
2
The tool check. He asks whether you are using the tools you've been given (the AP They Say / I Say Sentence Templates + Transitions document).

"How are your verbs? How are your transitions? Are you moving writing tools and lessons into your writing?" (sent 9/11)  |  "are you going back to this document and using these templates and transitions?… Why are you or why aren't you?" ("For Tuesday (9/15)" email, sent 9/14)

Your move: actually open the documents and use them while you write.
3
Notes are developed, not summary. He says notes should go past summary into analysis, interpretation, and synthesis.

"The aim of your developed notes should move beyond summary and into ANALYSIS, INTERPRETATION, and SYNTHESIS.… In other words, notes of summary will result in a conversation of summary. Your Constitution should demand more." ("For Friday (9/11)" email, sent 9/10)

Your move: in your notes, point to a sentence that is clearly your thinking, not a retelling.
4
The task, lettered. The work comes in lettered sections (A, B in the 9/11 email; A, B, C with C1–C4 in the 9/14 email). In the 9/14 email, section A was the foundation:

"Learn this as the base so that I can build upon what you are learning here… Learn the basics so that I can teach the advanced." ("For Tuesday (9/15)" email, sent 9/14, section A)

Your move: do the early letters for real — they set up the later ones.
5
Reflect on the process. The 9/14 email closed with a reflective (rhetorical) question about whether you used the tools — the same email talks about "your thinking about your thinking."

"A quick reflective (rhetorical) question: How did you use your documents and lists spoken about in the introductory section of "A)" and "B)"? (No writing is needed here as you already completed the writing. Now complete the reflecting.)" (sent 9/14)

Your move: answer it honestly to yourself. Did the tools change how you wrote?

The signals to watch for

When you see any of these, he's making one of the five moves above:

  • "read carefully and fully… context, feedback, and opportunity for curiosity" → move 1 (read it all).
  • "How are your verbs? How are your transitions?" / "using these templates and transitions?" → move 2 (use the tools).
  • "notes of summary" / "ANALYSIS, INTERPRETATION, and SYNTHESIS" → move 3 (develop your notes).
  • "Learn the basics so that I can teach the advanced" / lettered A, B, C → move 4 (don't skip the foundation).
  • "Why are you or why aren't you?" / "Now complete the reflecting" → move 5 (reflect honestly).
  • "Let us meet here again - Socrates." How these emails (and most of his others) sign off.

Try it: a playbook for any of his assignments

Our study suggestion, built from the moves above.

  1. Read the entire email first — the opening note and the reflection, not just the lettered task.
  2. Do the early letters for real; open the documents he names and use them.
  3. Make room for your voice and position (9/14: "Where are you finding space for YOUR voice and YOUR position within this style of writing?").
  4. Use the tools he names: the They Say / I Say templates + transitions, "appeals to" language, the Glossary of Terms.
  5. Answer his reflective question honestly before you hit reply.
