Study guide

Part 1 — Intro to Anatomy & Physiology

  • Anatomy = the study of the body's shape and structure. Physiology = the study of how it functions. The link: structure determines function — a part's shape is why it can do its job (a bowl is deep so liquid stays in; a plate is flat so you can cut).
  • Levels of organization (small → big): chemical (atoms) → molecules → organelles → cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism. Memory hook: Crazy Monkeys Only Chase Tigers On Open Savannas.
  • Survival needs: nutrients (carbs = fuel, proteins = building material, fats = cushion + reserve fuel), oxygen (~20% of air), water (60–89% of body weight).
  • Necessary life functions — be able to list them: maintaining boundaries, movement, responsiveness, digestion, respiration (O2 in / CO2 out), metabolism, excretion, growth, reproduction. Trick fact: reproduction is the one NOT needed for an individual to survive — only for the species.

Homeostasis — the big idea of the unit

  • Homeostasis = keeping internal conditions constant (temperature, pH, blood glucose, water, ions, pressure, gases). If the body can't maintain it, you die — that's why it matters.
  • The loop, in order: Stimulus (something changes) → Receptor (detects it) → Control center (brain — decides) → Effector (muscle or gland — responds).
  • Two control speeds: nervous system = fast (nerve impulses), endocrine system = slow (hormones).
  • Negative feedback reverses the change → keeps things stable → this IS homeostasis. Examples: body temperature (sweat/shiver), blood glucose, blood pressure, pulse. Sign pattern: + → − → +.
  • Positive feedback amplifies the change → pushes further from the start point → NOT homeostatic, ends only when the event finishes. Examples: childbirth (contractions build until birth), blood clotting, sneezing. Sign pattern: + → + → +.
  • Fast test: ask "does the response oppose the change or add to it?" Oppose = negative. Add = positive.

Anatomical position, directions, planes, cavities

  • Anatomical position: standing erect, facing forward, feet flat and forward, arms at sides, palms facing forward. Every directional term assumes this pose — that's why the thumb is LATERAL to the fingers.
  • Directional pairs (learn as opposites): superior (toward the head) / inferior (away from it); anterior/ventral (front) / posterior/dorsal (back); medial (toward the midline) / lateral (away from it); proximal (nearer the limb's attachment) / distal (farther from it); superficial (toward the surface) / deep (away from it). Proximal/distal are for limbs only.
  • Planes: sagittal = left/right (midsagittal = equal halves, parasagittal = unequal); frontal (coronal) = front/back; transverse = top/bottom.
  • Cavities:
    Dorsal (back)Cranial — brain  ·  Vertebral/spinal — spinal cord
    Ventral (front)Thoracic — heart + lungs  ·  Abdominopelvic = abdominal (stomach, liver, spleen, gallbladder, small intestine, most of large intestine) + pelvic (bladder, rest of large intestine, internal reproductive organs). No wall between abdominal and pelvic.

Part 2 — Cells

  • Cell theory: (1) all organisms are made of cells, (2) the cell is the basic unit of organization, (3) all cells come from pre-existing cells.
  • Key structures + jobs: cell membrane — the boundary, selectively permeable (its structure lets some things through and blocks others — structure → function again); nucleus — control center, holds DNA, its nucleolus builds ribosomes; mitochondria — make ATP (energy); ribosomes — build proteins; cytoplasm — supports the organelles.
  • Genetic information: DNA is the blueprint. Genes → proteins → the cell's identity (why a skin cell ≠ a neuron). Protein synthesis happens at the ribosomes, from amino acid building blocks. Radiation and chemicals can damage DNA; hormones change which proteins get made.
  • ATP: the usable energy currency. Cellular respiration releases it from glucose: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + 38 ATP. No ATP = no cell processes.
  • Cell life cycle: interphase (grow + copy DNA) → Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase → cytokinesis (split). Hook: PMAT. Mitosis = body cells (growth, repair, replacement); meiosis = sex cells.
  • Cancer = cells that lost growth control — normal cells stop dividing on contact with neighbors; cancer cells don't.

Part 3 — Tissues

  • Histology = the study of tissues. A tissue = similar cells sharing structure + function. There are exactly four types.
WhereJobKnow this
Epithelialcovers surfaces, lines cavities and organsprotection, barrier, secretion, absorptionavascular (no blood vessels); cells tightly packed; ~90% of cancers start here
Connectiveeverywhere — under skin, around organssupport, binding, protection, transport, energy storagemost varied family: loose (padding), fibrous (tendons), blood, adipose (fat), cartilage, bone
Muscleattached to bone, organ walls, heartmovement3 types — skeletal (voluntary), cardiac (heart, involuntary), smooth (hollow organs, involuntary)
Nervousbrain, spinal cord, nervescontrol + communicationneurons (dendrites, cell body, axon) + glial cells (support crew); neurons barely regenerate
  • Epithelium is named shape × layers: shape — squamous (squished flat), cuboidal (cubes), columnar (tall columns); layers — simple (one layer, where things pass through) vs stratified (many layers, where protection matters). So "stratified squamous" = many layers of flat cells (like your skin).
  • Connective tissue types + jobs: embryonic = stem cells; loose = padding; fibrous = dense + elastic (tendons); blood = transport (yes, blood IS connective tissue — favorite trick question); adipose = fat (cushion + energy); cartilage = padding between bones; bone = dense + spongy support.
  • Germ layers (~18 days after fertilization, 3-layer disc that all tissues come from): endoderm (inner) → digestive linings; mesoderm (middle) → muscle, bone, blood vessels; ectoderm (outer) → skin surface + nervous system.

Common traps

  • Anatomy vs physiology: anatomy = parts, physiology = how they work. If the question says "function," it's physiology.
  • Positive feedback is not "good" and negative is not "bad" — negative = stabilizing (homeostasis), positive = amplifying (childbirth, clotting).
  • Thumb is lateral, not medial — anatomical position has palms forward.
  • Proximal/distal only work on limbs; use superior/inferior on the trunk and head.
  • Blood is a connective tissue; epithelial tissue has no blood vessels.
  • Cardiac vs smooth muscle: both involuntary — cardiac is ONLY the heart; smooth is hollow organs (stomach, vessels, bladder).

Practice quiz

Part A — Intro & homeostasis (multiple choice)

1

Physiology is the study of: a) the body's structures b) how the body functions c) tissues only d) disease

Show answer

b — how the body functions. (Anatomy = structure.)

2

Which lists the levels of organization from smallest to largest? a) cell → organelle → tissue → organ b) chemical → cell → organelle → tissue c) chemical → organelle → cell → tissue → organ → organ system d) tissue → cell → organ → system

Show answer

c — atoms/chemical → molecules → organelles → cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.

3

Which life function is NOT necessary for an individual's survival? a) excretion b) responsiveness c) reproduction d) metabolism

Show answer

c — reproduction. Needed for the species, not the individual.

4

Put the homeostatic loop in order: a) receptor → stimulus → effector → control center b) stimulus → receptor → control center → effector c) control center → stimulus → receptor → effector d) stimulus → effector → receptor → control center

Show answer

b — stimulus → receptor (detects) → control center (brain) → effector (muscle or gland).

5

Shivering when your body temperature drops is an example of: a) positive feedback b) negative feedback c) metabolism d) excretion

Show answer

b — negative feedback: the response (making heat) reverses the change (falling temp).

6

Which is a positive feedback example? a) blood glucose regulation b) body temperature c) childbirth contractions d) blood pressure

Show answer

c — childbirth: each contraction triggers stronger ones until the baby is born. (Also: clotting, sneezing.)

7

Fast responses come from the ______ system; slow ones from the ______ system. a) endocrine / nervous b) nervous / endocrine c) muscular / skeletal d) nervous / cardiovascular

Show answer

b — nervous = fast (impulses), endocrine = slow (hormones).

8

Which plane divides the body into anterior and posterior portions? a) sagittal b) transverse c) frontal (coronal) d) midsagittal

Show answer

c — frontal/coronal = front vs back. Sagittal = left/right; transverse = top/bottom.

9

Part C — Cells (multiple choice)

16

Which pairing is wrong? a) nucleus — control center b) ribosomes — protein synthesis c) mitochondria — makes ATP d) cell membrane — lets everything through freely

Show answer

d — the membrane is selectively permeable: it controls what passes, it doesn't let everything through.

17

ATP matters to the cell because it: a) stores the genetic code b) is the usable form of energy for cell processes c) builds the membrane d) carries oxygen

Show answer

b — respiration breaks glucose down to release energy as ATP (38 per glucose); every cell process runs on it.

18

The correct order of mitosis phases is: a) metaphase → prophase → anaphase → telophase b) prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase c) prophase → anaphase → metaphase → telophase d) telophase → anaphase → metaphase → prophase

Show answer

b — PMAT (with interphase before and cytokinesis after).

19

"Genetic information" means: a) the proteins in the cytoplasm b) the DNA code that determines the cell's proteins and characteristics c) the number of mitochondria d) ATP stores

Show answer

b — DNA (genes) is the blueprint; it's copied when cells divide and read to build proteins that give each cell its identity.

20

Cancer is best described as: a) cells dividing too slowly b) cells that lost the ability to control their growth rate c) a virus d) cells with extra mitochondria

Show answer

b — normal cells stop dividing on contact with neighbors; cancer cells keep going until nutrients run out.

Part D — Tissues (matching)

21

Match each tissue to its description:

1. epithelial   2. connective   3. muscle   4. nervous

A. supports, binds, transports — includes bone, blood, and fat
B. covers surfaces and lines cavities; avascular; where ~90% of cancers start
C. communicates via electrical impulses; neurons + glial cells
D. contracts to produce movement

Show answer

1→B, 2→A, 3→D, 4→C.

22

"Stratified squamous epithelium" means: a) one layer of cube cells b) many layers of flat cells c) one layer of tall cells d) many layers of column cells

Show answer

b — stratified = many layers, squamous = flat/squished. That's your skin surface: layered for protection.

23

Which muscle type is voluntary? a) cardiac b) smooth c) skeletal d) all three

Show answer

c — skeletal (attached to bone, you choose to move it). Cardiac = heart only; smooth = hollow organs; both involuntary.

24

Which of these is a connective tissue? a) blood b) skin surface c) neurons d) heart muscle

Show answer

a — blood. The classic trick question: its cells (red, white, platelets) sit in a fluid matrix, which is the connective-tissue pattern.

25

The mesoderm germ layer gives rise to: a) digestive linings b) muscle, bone, and blood vessels c) skin and nervous system d) hair and nails

Show answer

b — meso (middle) → muscle/bone/vessels. Endo (inner) → digestive linings; ecto (outer) → skin + nervous system.

Part E — Short answer (explain it like you'd teach it)

26

Define homeostasis, then walk one real example through all four loop components (stimulus → receptor → control center → effector).

Show answer

Strong answer: Homeostasis is the body keeping internal conditions constant. Example — body temperature: you overheat in the sun (stimulus), temperature receptors in the skin detect it (receptor), the brain compares it to the set point (control center), and sweat glands activate to cool you down (effector). The response reverses the change → negative feedback.

27

Explain "structure determines function" using one body example.

Show answer

Any solid example works: e.g., simple squamous epithelium in the lungs' air sacs is a single layer of flat cells — thin because gases must pass through quickly. Or: the cell membrane's structure makes it selectively permeable. Name the structure, name the job, connect them with "because."

28

Positive and negative feedback: define both, give one example of each, and say which one maintains homeostasis.

Show answer

Negative reverses a change (body temp, blood glucose, blood pressure) — this is the one that maintains homeostasis. Positive amplifies a change until an event completes (childbirth, blood clotting, sneezing) — stabilizing it is not; it's for finishing a job fast.
