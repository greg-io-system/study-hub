MLA format, Mr. Evans's way

His template is a short version of MLA. Here's exactly what it asks for, what it looks like on the page, and how to set it up in Google Docs. Use it for every paper in this class.

The big idea: Mr. Evans says he doesn't grade formatting — but a sloppy page costs you ethos before he reads a word. Clean formatting is free credibility. It takes five minutes, once.

The pieces, one at a time

HeaderTop right of every page: your last name and the page number, like Lastname 1. The number counts up by itself.
HeadingTop left of page 1 only, four lines: your full name, Mr. Evans, AP Language and Composition, the due date.
DateDay, month, year, no commas: 15 October 2026 — not October 15, 2026 or 10/15/26. Use the due date, not the day you wrote it.
TitleCentered, plain text (no bold, no underline, no quotation marks). His rule: name your paper, not the assignment. Position Paper #1 tells him nothing; a title that hints at your position does.
FontTimes New Roman, 12 pt, for everything — header, heading, title, body.
SpacingDouble-spaced everywhere, including the heading. No extra blank lines between paragraphs.
ParagraphsIndent the first line half an inch (one Tab). Margins stay at 1 inch (the Docs default).
Works CitedNot needed in this class. Your sources are introduced inside your sentences instead (next section).

Set it up in Google Docs

  1. Font: select all (Ctrl+A), choose Times New Roman and 12.
  2. Spacing: still selected, Format → Line & paragraph spacing → Double. In the same menu, make sure "Add space before/after paragraph" is off.
  3. Header: Insert → Page numbers → pick the top-right option. Click just before the number in the header and type your last name and a space. Set the header to Times New Roman 12 too.
  4. Heading: type the four lines at the top left, pressing Enter after each.
  5. Title: type it on the next line, then Center it (Ctrl+Shift+E). Press Enter and set the next line back to Left (Ctrl+Shift+L).
  6. Start writing — press Tab at the start of each paragraph.

Shortcut: Mr. Evans's own MLA Template is linked on the hub card. Its "How to make a header/page #" box shows the same Format/Insert menus.

Citing sources without a Works Cited

Because there's no Works Cited page, the first time you use a source, your sentence does the citing. It's the same opening move as the rhetorical précis:

Author + title + (year) + a strong verb (argues, claims, suggests, asserts) + that + their main point — and if you quote, the page number in parentheses at the end.

A made-up example of the pattern:

In “Why Boredom Is Good for You” (2019), Sam Rivera argues that “a little nothing to do is where ideas start” (3).
  • Titles of articles and essays go in quotation marks; titles of whole books (like Freakonomics) go in italics.
  • The (3) is the page the quote came from. Online articles have no page numbers — just leave it off.
  • The period goes after the parentheses: …start” (3).
  • After the first mention, use the author's last name only: “Rivera's point is…”
