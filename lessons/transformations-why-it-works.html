Algebra 2 · Transformations

Why It Works

Not more formulas — the picture behind them. Read this once and the worksheet stops being memorization and starts being obvious.

The one idea

📌 A function is a rubber stamp

Here's the whole secret: transformations never invent a new shape. A parabola is always a parabola; a V is always a V. You already own the shape. All the equation is doing is telling you where to press the stamp and how hard.

The parent function — plain x², |x|, √x, x — is the stamp itself, pressed dead-center at the origin. Then three knobs move and resize that stamp:

k slides it up/down · h slides it left/right · a stretches or flips it

That's it. The rest of this page is just why each knob does what it does — so you never have to memorize the rules, you can rebuild them.

The easy knob

↕️ k is an elevator

+ k at the very end runs the machine first, then lifts every single output by k. The whole graph rides an elevator up (if k is positive) or down (if k is negative).

Nothing subtle here — this is the honest knob. +3 means up 3. It means what it says because it happens last, to the answer. Hold that thought: things that happen to the answer are vertical and honest. We'll use it in a second.

The loud knob

🔊 a is a volume knob — and a mirror

Multiplying the output by a exaggerates it. Turn it past 1 and every height gets louder — the parabola shoots up steeper, the V gets sharper. Turn it below 1 (like 0.5) and everything gets quieter — the shape flattens out.

And a negative a is a mirror laid on the x-axis. Every point that was above flips to the same distance below. An upside-down parabola isn't a new rule — it's the same parabola with −1 flipping it over the water line.

Notice: a multiplies the output too, so like the elevator it's a vertical effect. It changes how tall and which way up — never left or right.

The famous mystery

🏃 Why does (x − 3) move RIGHT?

This is the one that makes everybody feel like math is lying to them. It isn't. Here's the honest reason.

Every one of these shapes has a landmark — the vertex of a parabola, the tip of a V, the start of a square root. The parent hits its landmark when the inside equals zero. Plain x² hits bottom at x = 0, because that's when the inside (just x) is 0.

Now feed the machine (x − 3). It still wants its inside to be zero to hit the landmark. But now the inside is only zero when x = 3. So the landmark doesn't happen until you've walked out to x = 3 — three steps to the right.

The runner picture: imagine the graph is a runner who started 3 meters behind the start line. To watch them cross the line, you look further down the track — to the right. x − 3 is a runner who's running late, so the whole picture shifts right to catch up with them.

So the sign isn't backwards at all. You're solving inside = 0, and x − 3 = 0 gives x = 3. The minus makes a plus when you solve it. x + 4 = 0 gives x = −4 → left 4. Same logic every time.

The pattern under all of it: anything inside the parentheses acts on your input — it happens before the machine runs, it's horizontal, and it feels backwards because you have to undo it (solve for x). Anything outside acts on your output — it happens after, it's vertical, and it's honest. Inside vs outside. Before vs after. That single split explains every transformation you'll ever see.

Domain & range, felt not memorized

🧱 Where the graph is allowed to live

Why a square root has a wall. You cannot take the square root of a negative number — the machine literally jams. So √(x − 3) refuses to produce anything until the inside stops being negative, i.e. until x reaches 3. That's not a domain rule to memorize; it's the machine breaking to the left of its start point. The wall is at x = h for the same reason the landmark was.

Why range flips with a. The landmark is the shape's resting floor. A right-side-up parabola or V sits on its vertex and has nowhere to go but up → the outputs start at k and climb. Flip it with a negative a and that floor becomes a ceiling — now it hangs down from k. So "opens up → [k, ∞), opens down → (−∞, k]" isn't two rules; it's one picture and which way it faces.

So what

The worksheet, re-read

When a problem says f(x) = −2(x − 1)² + 3, you're not decoding a formula. You're reading a sentence: "take the parabola stamp, press it at (1, 3), turn the volume up to 2, and flip it upside-down."

Every blank on the sheet is just describing that press:

  • Parent = which stamp.
  • Vertex / start = where you pressed it → (h, k).
  • Transformations = louder? flipped? which way slid?
  • Domain / range = where the shape is allowed to live.

Read the sentence. Press the stamp. Fill the blanks.
